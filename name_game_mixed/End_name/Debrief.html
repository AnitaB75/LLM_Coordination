Debriefing: Coordination games and cooperative matching

1. What was this study about?

This study investigates how people coordinate their choices in cooperative matching games where groups of 6 players must all select the same letter to achieve successful coordination.

2. How was the study conducted?

All participants were recruited via Prolific. They played a 14-round letter coordination game where they tried to choose the same letter as all other players in their group from a set of 10 possible letters (J, P, Q, R, N, M, F, C, X, Y). In rounds 1–7, only J awarded points (10 pts); in rounds 8–14, J (10 pts), M (15 pts), and N (15 pts) could all award points if the whole group coordinated on the same letter. Groups stayed fixed throughout all rounds.

3. What was the hypothesis?

We predict that coordination success will depend on how players develop shared strategies for letter selection within their group. We are interested in understanding how coordination emerges through repeated interactions and how information feedback affects cooperation. In this study, we are also interested in how the presence of an AI teammate influences human coordination strategies.

5. Did we tell you everything?

Yes, all aspects of the game worked exactly as described. We are studying coordination patterns to understand how shared strategies emerge through repeated interactions.

6. Why is this study important?

Understanding how people coordinate without explicit communication has important implications for teamwork, collaboration, and decision-making in various contexts where groups must work together to achieve shared goals. This research also helps us understand how information sharing affects cooperation. More broadly, as AI agents become increasingly present in collaborative settings, understanding how humans and AI coordinate — and how human behaviour is shaped by the presence of an AI — is of growing scientific and societal relevance.
